Game Overview

You play as the Girl Driller, a lone space miner drilling deeper and deeper into a series of asteroid mining zones. Your goal is simple: fly around, destroy mineable objects (asteroids), collect the minerals and Antique Power Up they drop, and use the money to unlock and upgrade an ever-growing arsenal of Power-Ups and Overdrive abilities.

As you spend Money in the Skill Tree to unlock more Power-Ups, new Mining Zones unlock further out from Earth, each one adding tougher, denser fields of mineable objects - see the Zones page for exactly how many Power-Ups each zone needs, and the Extra Survival Levels page for the survival challenge that stands between each pair of zones. Progression is driven entirely by the Skill Tree: money earned from mining and combat is spent there to unlock new Power-Ups, boost their damage/fire rate, and extend how long each Overdrive stays active.

Player Controls

  • Move: Arrow Keys or W / A / S / D. Movement steadily drains your Energy meter - you can only move while you have Energy left.
  • Move (mouse/touch): Press and hold the left mouse button, or touch and hold the screen, and the ship thrusts toward that point. On touch devices you can also drag anywhere to move.
  • Attacking: There is no manual fire button - every unlocked Power-Up (Drill, Laser, Fireball, Rocket, Drone, etc.) fires automatically on its own, at its own pace, once you've unlocked it in the Skill Tree.
  • Overdrive buttons: Each unlocked Power-Up gets its own Overdrive button in the on-screen Active Systems panel. Tap/click a button once it reads READY to activate that Overdrive.
  • Pause: Click/tap the Pause button in the HUD, or press Escape.
  • Menus: Enter confirms/continues on menu and story screens; Escape backs out of the Skill Tree or closes the pause menu.
  • Mobile: All of the above works the same way with on-screen taps - the Overdrive buttons and Pause button are always visible in the HUD, and touch-and-hold anywhere on the play area to move.

Mining & Destroyable Objects

Every Mining Zone is filled with destroyable objects (asteroids). They sit in place until a Power-Up's projectile or effect strikes them.

  • Spawning: Objects continually spawn back into the zone as they're destroyed, drawn from that zone's own pool of mineral types.
  • Movement: Objects are stationary - they don't chase or move toward you; you fly out to them.
  • Damage from Power-Ups: Any hit from any active Power-Up's projectile registers as exactly one hit against that object's fixed hit requirement (see the durability table below) - a Power-Up's damage/upgrade level does not let a single hit count as more than one hit.
  • HP / hit system: Each object has a fixed number of hits it can take before being destroyed, based purely on its mineral type (Iron/Bronze/Gold/Diamond/Uranium) - see below.
  • 5 damage frames: Every object's art has 5 stages, from full health down to almost destroyed, so you get a clear visual cue of how close an object is to breaking apart as you keep landing hits.
  • On destruction: The object breaks apart and is removed, and a batch of that mineral is released for you to fly over and collect (see Minerals & Rewards below), and a new object eventually spawns to replace it.

Minerals & Rewards

Destroying an object releases its mineral reward, which you collect simply by flying close enough to touch it - it's then added to your running Money total automatically. Each mineral type has its own Money value per unit and its own yield per destroy, and rarer minerals tend to take more hits to break loose - see the tables below.

Money is the currency spent on every Skill Tree unlock and upgrade, so mining efficiently (using the right Power-Ups for the zone you're in, and popping Overdrives on cooldown) directly speeds up your progression.

Antique Power Up

Separate from ordinary mineral drops, rare Antique Power Up collectibles occasionally spawn out in the zone as their own glowing pickups. Fly into one to collect it - it's instantly added to your Antique Power Up tally, shown as its own icon grid in the HUD with a running count for each type collected. There are 2 different Antique Power Up types to find and collect: Dragon Skull and Flower.

This isn't just a passive tally: the first time you've collected 100 of both types, a powerful Dragon Skull Fire Attack triggers automatically - twin dragon skulls appear and breathe fire across the zone on their own for a short time, with no button or Overdrive slot of its own. Once unlocked this way, it keeps firing again automatically every 15 minutes for the rest of the game, and every Dragon Skull/Flower you collect afterward keeps counting - it never stops or resets.

Power-Ups

Power-Ups are unlocked one at a time from the Skill Tree by spending Money. Once unlocked, a Power-Up fires automatically for the rest of the run/session - you never have to aim or trigger it manually. Every combat Power-Up also has its own paired Overdrive (see the Overdrives section below) and its own upgrade nodes that boost its damage, fire rate, or other stats.

Drilling (starting Power-Up)

Your basic mining tool, available from the very start with no unlock cost. Automatically drills into nearby objects. Upgraded through the Drilling tree (Tungsten/Plasma/Diamond/Void Drill nodes) for more damage. Its Overdrive is Drill Storm.

Girl Suit / Nano-Suit

Unlocks the "Girl Power" suit ability and is also the gateway node most other Power-Ups are unlocked behind. Suit upgrades add Armor (reduced damage taken), a Magnetic Suit (collect minerals from farther away without flying right up to them), and a late-game Cosmic Suit for maximum protection. Its Overdrive is Girl Power.

Impact Pickaxe

A melee-range hand weapon unlocked from the Suit tree. Its Overdrive, Axe Fury, spawns two Pixel Axes that swing and strike nearby objects for the duration of the Overdrive.

Missiles (Mining Rocket)

Fires homing rockets automatically at nearby objects at a steady reload rate (upgradeable for faster reloading). Its Overdrive is Missile Rain.

Laser Weapon (Plasma Laser Cannon)

Fires a directional laser beam automatically at objects. Its Overdrive, Laser Beam, summons four Plasma Laser Cannons positioned diagonally around you (up-left, up-right, down-left, down-right), all firing simultaneously at every object in range for the Overdrive's duration.

Fireball

Auto-fires fireball projectiles at objects. Its Overdrive is Inferno.

Attack Drone

A companion drone that flies alongside you and fires its own shots automatically. Upgradeable for more projectile damage (Twin Cannons) and faster fire rate (Rapid Targeting). Its Overdrive is Drone Swarm.

Power-Ups (continued)

Flower Bomb

A rotating flower bomb that seeks out nearby objects and detonates on contact for explosion damage. Upgradeable for more explosion damage and a longer target-seeking range. Its Overdrive is also named Flower Bomb.

Pulse Core

Emits damaging energy shockwaves outward from your ship automatically. Upgradeable for more damage and a larger shockwave radius. Its Overdrive is Core Overload.

Electric Shock

Automatically zaps nearby objects with electricity. Upgradeable for more damage and faster attack speed. Its Overdrive is Electric Storm.

Fire Split

Fires three independent fire projectiles at once - one left, one forward, one right - each traveling and hitting separately. Upgradeable for more damage and a faster throwing rate. Its Overdrive, Firestorm Split, repeatedly fires bursts of nine independent projectiles (three groups of three) for as long as the Overdrive is active.

Laser Drone

Deploys a Drone that automatically fires Laser bolts at objects. Upgradeable for more damage and faster firing speed. Its Overdrive is Laser Drone.

Collector

Not a weapon - this unlocks a small autonomous companion that automatically flies to and gathers whatever minerals/Antique Power Up collectibles are currently visible on screen, then moves on to the next one. It never attacks and has no Overdrive; it just saves you legwork on pickups.

Power-Up Upgrades

Every combat Power-Up has its own branch in the Skill Tree with numbered upgrade nodes (most go up to level 20 or 25). Spending Money on these nodes increases that Power-Up's damage, fire rate, throwing/attack speed, or a similar stat depending on the Power-Up. A fully upgraded Power-Up hits harder and fires faster than a freshly-unlocked one.

Important: upgrading a Power-Up never bypasses a destroyable object's hit requirement. A fresh, unupgraded Power-Up needs 3 hits to break Iron, 6 for Bronze, 9 for Gold, 10 for Diamond, and 12 for Uranium; a fully maxed-out Power-Up needs only 2/4/6/8/10 respectively - upgrades progressively get you there faster and let you fight tougher zones more comfortably.

Overdrives

Every unlocked Power-Up has a matching Overdrive - a temporary, supercharged burst of that ability. Overdrives are entirely time-based: they do not cost minerals or Money to activate. Instead each one cycles through a Wait phase and an Active phase on its own timer.

  • Wait: After an Overdrive ends (or when a Power-Up is first unlocked), its button shows a countdown, e.g. "WAIT: 5s". You cannot activate it during this phase.
  • Ready: Once the wait timer hits zero the button turns to READY - the Overdrive does not fire on its own. You must tap/click its button to activate it.
  • Active: Once activated, that Power-Up gets a temporary boost (extra projectiles, extra companions, larger effects, etc. - see the per-Overdrive notes above) for exactly 10 seconds, every single activation.
  • Next cycle: When the Active phase ends, that Overdrive's use count increases by one and a new, longer Wait phase begins immediately.
  • Multiple ready at once: If more than one Overdrive becomes READY at the same time, pressing any of their buttons opens a selection screen listing every ready Overdrive as a card - you pick exactly one to activate, and the others stay fully charged and ready until you activate them next.
  • Overdrive Core upgrades: Each Power-Up has its own Overdrive Core upgrade node in the Skill Tree that adds a flat +1 second to that Overdrive's Active phase, on top of the base 10 seconds, for every level purchased.

Overdrive Timing

The Active phase is always exactly 10 seconds, on every activation - it never grows. Only the Wait/recharge phase that follows grows, by 5 seconds every time you use that Overdrive:

  • 1st use: Wait 5s (base) → Active 10s
  • 2nd use: Wait 10s → Active 10s
  • 3rd use: Wait 15s → Active 10s
  • ...and so on - Wait keeps growing by 5 seconds per use, Active always stays 10s.

Overdrive Core upgrade levels add flat bonus seconds on top of the 10-second Active phase shown above (not the Wait phase).

Hazards

Mining Zones aren't just full of destroyable objects - a roaming Black Hole drifts through every zone as an environmental hazard.

  • Behavior: The Black Hole drifts slowly around the zone on its own. It never chases you outright, but its pull grows the closer you get.
  • Capture: Fly too close and it can pull your ship in, dragging you into a helpless orbit around it for a few seconds before releasing you at a safe location elsewhere in the zone.
  • Warning: A "DANGER: BLACK HOLE" label appears above it, so you always have a visual cue to steer clear.
  • It deals no direct damage - the danger is losing control of your ship and being repositioned mid-fight, not health loss.
This is separate from the Antique Power Up's Dragon Skull Fire Attack (see Minerals & Rewards) and from the giant Black Hole boss fought at the end of each Extra Survival Level (see the next page) - all three use similar art, but are independent systems.

Zones

There are 5 main Mining Zones, plus a 6th final zone unlocked much later. Zones unlock based on how many Power-Ups you've unlocked in total (not Depth or Money) - and every zone after the first also requires clearing that zone's preceding Extra Survival Level (see the next page). Each zone adds its own set of destroyable objects into the mix, and every object unlocked in an earlier zone stays available in every later zone too - so later zones have a larger, more varied mix of objects rather than replacing what came before.

You can travel between unlocked zones from the Mining Zones menu (accessible from the Pause menu). A zone whose Power-Up requirement is met but whose Extra Survival Level isn't finished yet shows a "Complete the Extra Level Survival Challenge" requirement instead of a Travel button.

Extra Survival Levels

Between each pair of Mining Zones sits a mandatory Extra Survival Level - a separate, self-contained combat challenge that launches automatically the moment you reach the Power-Up requirement for the next zone. There are 5 Extra Survival Levels in total, one built from each of Zones 1-5's own destroyable objects.

  • You fly a Jet, not the Girl: this mode temporarily swaps you into a fighter jet with its own auto-firing forward bolts and homing wing missiles - none of your normal Power-Ups, Overdrives, or the Skill Tree are used here.
  • Hearts: you have 3 Hearts. Colliding with a hazard costs a Heart (with a brief invulnerability window afterward); losing all 3 fails the level.
  • Survive phase: destroy the incoming objects and dodge hazards for a set time that grows by 5 minutes with every Extra Level (Extra Level 1 = 5 minutes, Level 2 = 10 minutes, and so on).
  • Boss phase: once the survive timer ends, a giant Black Hole boss appears with no time limit of its own - its Power doubles with every Extra Level (1,000 on Level 1, then 2,000, 4,000, 8,000, 16,000). Land enough jet-fire hits to bring its Power to 0 to win.
  • Reward: completing an Extra Survival Level pays out Money directly - $100,000 x that level's number (Level 1 = $100,000, Level 2 = $200,000, and so on) - and immediately sends you into the newly-unlocked next zone.
  • Not missable: if you exit without finishing, it isn't lost - a "⚔ SURVIVAL CHALLENGE" button appears next to that zone in the Mining Zones menu so you can retry it any time.

Collector System

Once unlocked from the Skill Tree, the Collector is a small companion ship that flies alongside you. It automatically seeks out whichever minerals/Antique Power Up collectibles are currently visible on screen and gathers them one after another, moving on to the next target once it grabs one. It never attacks or damages anything, and its own travel speed is fixed - no upgrade makes it faster. Its only job is to save you the trouble of flying over to every drop yourself, letting you focus on fighting and moving on to the next target.

Game Progression

  • Mine objects and collect minerals/Antique Power Up to build up Money.
  • Spend Money in the Skill Tree to unlock new Power-Ups, upgrade existing ones, and add levels to Overdrive Cores.
  • As your Power-Up count grows, new Mining Zones unlock - each one (after Zone 1) also requires beating that zone's preceding Extra Survival Level first.
  • Every unlocked combat Power-Up gets its own Overdrive, which strengthens on a growing wait/active cycle every time you use it.
  • Rewards (Money from minerals, Antique Power Up pickups, Extra Survival Level payouts, and Skill Tree progress) all build on each other - better Power-Ups let you clear tougher zones faster, which earns more Money, which funds more upgrades.
  • Fully unlocking and upgrading everything, alongside earning $100,000,000 lifetime, opens the final Zone 6: The Infinite Core.

Save System

The game uses full auto-save - there is no manual Save button and nothing you need to remember to do.

  • Your progress (Money, unlocked Power-Ups, upgrade levels, unlocked zones, completed Extra Survival Levels, Antique Power Up counts, and more) is saved automatically at key moments - pausing, leaving to the main menu, reaching a milestone, or traveling to a zone - and periodically during play.
  • Saved progress is loaded automatically the next time you open the game.
  • From the main menu, Continue resumes your saved game exactly where you left off.
  • New Game (shown once a save exists) and Reset Progress both wipe your saved progression back to a fresh start - use these only if you actually want to start over.

UI

  • Money: Your current spendable currency, shown in the HUD and used for every Skill Tree purchase.
  • Depth: How far you've mined/traveled, shown in the HUD and on the Mining Zones screen - a progress readout, but it does not by itself unlock new zones (that's based on your Power-Up count and Extra Survival Level completion instead).
  • Score: A running combat/progress score shown at the top of the HUD.
  • Power-Up / Overdrive buttons: The Active Systems panel lists every unlocked Power-Up with its own Overdrive button, showing WAIT/READY status and a countdown.
  • Antique Power Up panel: Shows one icon per Antique Power Up type with your collected count for each.
  • Energy bar: Shows your remaining Energy, which drains as you move and limits how long you can fly before needing to let it recover.
  • Pause button: Opens the pause menu, giving access to the Skill Tree, Mining Zones, and Main Menu.

Tips

  • Don't let Overdrives sit idle once they're READY - activating them regularly is the fastest way to clear tougher objects and keep their cycle (and their duration) growing.
  • If several Overdrives are ready at once, use the selection screen to pick the one that best matches what's in front of you right now (e.g. a wide area effect for a crowded field) - the rest stay charged for later.
  • Remember hits are fixed per mineral type (3/6/9/12) - upgrading damage speeds up how fast you land those hits, but tougher minerals will always take more hits than weaker ones.
  • Unlock the Collector early if you can afford it - letting it handle pickups frees you up to keep moving and fighting instead of detouring for every drop.
  • Watch your Energy bar while moving - running out mid-flight leaves you stranded until it recovers, so don't stray too far from the action.
  • Prioritize unlocking a couple of Power-Ups you like over spreading Money thin across many - a well-upgraded Power-Up and its Overdrive will carry you through a zone faster than several barely-upgraded ones.
  • Progress through zones as your current loadout allows - each new zone's objects stack on top of the previous zone's, so a stronger loadout makes earlier objects trivial while you focus on the newest threats.
  • Don't put off a Survival Challenge - the Extra Survival Level standing between you and the next zone only gets tougher (longer survive timer, tankier Black Hole boss) if you wait, and it's never removed from the Mining Zones menu.
