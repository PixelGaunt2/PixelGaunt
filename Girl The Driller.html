Game Overview

You play as the Girl Driller, a lone space miner working the Asteroid Belt. Your goal is simple: fly around, destroy mineable objects (asteroids), collect the minerals and Antique Cargo they drop, and use the money to unlock and upgrade an ever-growing arsenal of Power-Ups and Overdrive abilities.

As you earn Money, progression is driven entirely by the Skill Tree: money earned from mining and combat is spent there to unlock new Power-Ups, boost their damage/fire rate, and extend how long each Overdrive stays active.

Player Controls

  • Move: Arrow Keys or W / A / S / D. Movement steadily drains your Energy meter - you can only move while you have Energy left.
  • Move (mouse/touch): Press and hold the left mouse button, or touch and hold the screen, and the ship thrusts toward that point. On touch devices you can also drag anywhere to move.
  • Attacking: There is no manual fire button - every unlocked Power-Up (Drill, Suit, Fireball) fires automatically on its own, at its own pace, once you've unlocked it in the Skill Tree.
  • Overdrive buttons: Each of the 3 Power-Ups gets its own Overdrive button in the on-screen Overdrive panel. Tap/click a button once it reads READY to activate that Overdrive.
  • Pause: Click/tap the Pause button in the HUD, or press Escape.
  • Menus: Enter confirms/continues on menu and story screens; Escape backs out of the Skill Tree or closes the pause menu.
  • Mobile: All of the above works the same way with on-screen taps - the Overdrive buttons and Pause button are always visible in the HUD, and touch-and-hold anywhere on the play area to move.

Mining & Destroyable Objects

The Mining Zone is filled with destroyable objects (asteroids). They sit in place until a Power-Up's projectile or effect strikes them.

  • Spawning: Objects continually spawn back in as they're destroyed, drawn from the zone's pool of mineral types.
  • Movement: Objects are stationary - they don't chase or move toward you; you fly out to them.
  • Damage from Power-Ups: Any hit from any active Power-Up's projectile registers as exactly one hit against that object's fixed hit requirement (see the durability table below) - a Power-Up's damage/upgrade level does not let a single hit count as more than one hit.
  • HP / hit system: Each object has a fixed number of hits it can take before being destroyed, based purely on its mineral type (Iron/Bronze/Gold/Uranium) - see below.
  • 5 damage frames: Every object's art has 5 stages, from full health down to almost destroyed, so you get a clear visual cue of how close an object is to breaking apart as you keep landing hits.
  • On destruction: The object breaks apart and is removed, and a batch of that mineral is released for you to fly over and collect (see Minerals & Rewards below), and a new object eventually spawns to replace it.

Power-Up upgrades make your attacks hit harder per hit and fire faster, but they do not let one hit count as multiple hits - every object still needs its full fixed number of successful hits (3/6/9/12) to be destroyed, no matter how upgraded your Power-Ups are.

Minerals & Rewards

Destroying an object releases its mineral reward, which you collect simply by flying close enough to touch it - it's then added to your running Money total automatically. Higher-tier minerals (Gold, Uranium) are worth more Money per unit but take more hits to reach.

Money is the currency spent on every Skill Tree unlock and upgrade, so mining efficiently (using the right Power-Ups, and popping Overdrives on cooldown) directly speeds up your progression.

Antique Cargo

Separate from ordinary mineral drops, rare Antique Cargo collectibles occasionally spawn as their own glowing pickups. Fly into one to collect it - it's instantly added to your Antique Cargo tally, shown as its own icon grid in the HUD with a running count for each type collected. There are 5 different Antique Cargo mineral/crystal types to find and collect over the course of the game.

Power-Ups

Power-Ups are unlocked one at a time from the Skill Tree by spending Money. Once unlocked, a Power-Up fires automatically for the rest of the run/session - you never have to aim or trigger it manually. Drill, Suit, and Fireball each have their own paired Overdrive (see the Overdrives section below) and their own chain of upgrade nodes that boost damage, fire rate, or other stats.

Drilling (starting Power-Up)

Your basic mining tool, available from the very start with no unlock cost. Automatically drills into nearby objects. Upgraded through the Drill tree (Drill Power/Drill Speed/Heavy Drill/Max Drill nodes) for more damage, faster drill flight speed, and a longer Overdrive. Its Overdrive is Drill Storm.

Girl Suit / Nano-Suit

Unlocks the "Girl Power" suit ability and is also the gateway node the Fireball Power-Up is unlocked behind. Suit upgrades add Armor (reduced damage taken), Energy (larger energy pool), a Shield (extra damage reduction on top of Armor), and a capstone Max Suit node. Its Overdrive is Girl Power.

Fireball

Auto-fires fireball projectiles at objects. Upgraded through the Fireball tree (Fireball Power/Fire Rate/Fireball Size/Multi-Fire nodes) for more damage, faster throwing, a bigger orbit, and extra orbiting fireballs during its Overdrive. Its Overdrive is Inferno.

Power-Up Upgrades

Each of the 3 Power-Ups has its own branch in the Skill Tree with numbered upgrade nodes (up to level 20). Spending Money on these nodes increases that Power-Up's damage, fire rate, speed, or a similar stat depending on the Power-Up. A fully upgraded Power-Up hits harder and fires faster than a freshly-unlocked one.

Important: upgrading a Power-Up never bypasses a destroyable object's fixed hit requirement. A maxed-out Power-Up still needs 3 hits to break Iron, 6 for Bronze, 9 for Gold, and 12 for Uranium - upgrades just get you there faster and let you fight tougher objects more comfortably.

Collector

Not a weapon - this unlocks a small autonomous companion that automatically flies to and gathers whatever minerals/Antique Cargo collectibles are currently visible on screen, then moves on to the next one. It never attacks and has no Overdrive; it just saves you legwork on pickups.

Overdrives

Every unlocked Power-Up has a matching Overdrive - a temporary, supercharged burst of that ability. Overdrives are entirely time-based: they do not cost minerals or Money to activate. Instead each one cycles through a Wait phase and an Active phase on its own timer.

  • Wait: After an Overdrive ends (or when a Power-Up is first unlocked), its button shows a countdown, e.g. "WAIT: 5s". You cannot activate it during this phase.
  • Ready: Once the wait timer hits zero the button turns to READY - the Overdrive does not fire on its own. You must tap/click its button to activate it.
  • Active: Once activated, that Power-Up gets a temporary boost (extra projectiles, extra companions, larger effects, etc. - see the per-Overdrive notes above) for exactly 10 seconds, every single activation.
  • Next cycle: When the Active phase ends, that Overdrive's use count increases by one and a new, longer Wait phase begins immediately.
  • Multiple ready at once: If more than one Overdrive becomes READY at the same time, pressing any of their buttons opens a selection screen listing every ready Overdrive as a card - you pick exactly one to activate, and the others stay fully charged and ready until you activate them next.
  • Overdrive Core upgrades: Each Power-Up has its own Overdrive Core upgrade node in the Skill Tree that adds a flat +1 second to that Overdrive's Active phase, on top of the base 10 seconds, for every level purchased.

Overdrive Timing

The Active phase is always exactly 10 seconds, on every activation - it never grows. Only the Wait/recharge phase that follows grows, by 5 seconds every time you use that Overdrive:

  • 1st use: Wait 5s (base) → Active 10s
  • 2nd use: Wait 10s → Active 10s
  • 3rd use: Wait 15s → Active 10s
  • ...and so on - Wait keeps growing by 5 seconds per use, Active always stays 10s.

Overdrive Core upgrade levels add flat bonus seconds on top of the 10-second Active phase shown above (not the Wait phase).

The game takes place in a single Mining Zone: the Asteroid Belt. It has its own set of 5 destroyable objects, spawning endlessly as you clear them.

Collector System

Once unlocked from the Skill Tree, the Collector is a small companion ship that flies alongside you. It automatically seeks out whichever minerals/Antique Cargo collectibles are currently visible on screen and gathers them one after another, moving on to the next target once it grabs one. It never attacks or damages anything, and its own travel speed is fixed - no upgrade makes it faster. Its only job is to save you the trouble of flying over to every drop yourself, letting you focus on fighting and moving on to the next target.

Game Progression

  • Mine objects and collect minerals/Antique Cargo to build up Money.
  • Spend Money in the Skill Tree to unlock new Power-Ups, upgrade existing ones, and add levels to Overdrive Cores.
  • Every unlocked combat Power-Up gets its own Overdrive, which strengthens on a growing wait/active cycle every time you use it.
  • Rewards (Money from minerals, Antique Cargo pickups, and Skill Tree progress) all build on each other - better Power-Ups let you clear objects faster, which earns more Money, which funds more upgrades.

Save System

The game uses full auto-save - there is no manual Save button and nothing you need to remember to do.

  • Your progress (Money, unlocked Power-Ups, upgrade levels, Antique Cargo counts, and more) is saved automatically at key moments - pausing, leaving to the main menu, reaching a milestone - and periodically during play.
  • Saved progress is loaded automatically the next time you open the game.
  • From the main menu, Continue resumes your saved game exactly where you left off.
  • New Game (shown once a save exists) and Reset Progress both wipe your saved progression back to a fresh start - use these only if you actually want to start over.

UI

  • Money: Your current spendable currency, shown in the HUD and used for every Skill Tree purchase.
  • Depth: How far you've mined/traveled, shown in the HUD.
  • Score: A running combat/progress score shown at the top of the HUD.
  • Power-Up / Overdrive buttons: The Overdrive panel lists Drill, Suit, and Fireball with their own Overdrive button, showing WAIT/READY status and a countdown.
  • Antique Cargo panel: Shows one icon per Antique Cargo type with your collected count for each.
  • Energy bar: Shows your remaining Energy, which drains as you move and limits how long you can fly before needing to let it recover.
  • Pause button: Opens the pause menu, giving access to the Skill Tree and Main Menu.

Tips

  • Don't let Overdrives sit idle once they're READY - activating them regularly is the fastest way to clear tougher objects and keep their cycle (and their duration) growing.
  • If several Overdrives are ready at once, use the selection screen to pick the one that best matches what's in front of you right now (e.g. a wide area effect for a crowded field) - the rest stay charged for later.
  • Remember hits are fixed per mineral type (3/6/9/12) - upgrading damage speeds up how fast you land those hits, but tougher minerals will always take more hits than weaker ones.
  • Unlock the Collector early if you can afford it - letting it handle pickups frees you up to keep moving and fighting instead of detouring for every drop.
  • Watch your Energy bar while moving - running out mid-flight leaves you stranded until it recovers, so don't stray too far from the action.
  • Prioritize unlocking a couple of Power-Ups you like over spreading Money thin across many - a well-upgraded Power-Up and its Overdrive will carry you further than several barely-upgraded ones.
